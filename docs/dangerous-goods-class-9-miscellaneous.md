# Dangerous Goods — Class 9: Miscellaneous Dangerous Goods

Class 9 is a catch-all category for substances and articles that present a danger during transport but do not meet
the definition of any other hazard class. For Baobab Freight & Logistics, the two most common Class 9 categories are
lithium batteries and environmentally hazardous substances. This document supplements the Dangerous Goods Handling
Policy — Overview with Class 9-specific requirements.

## 1. Lithium batteries

Lithium-ion and lithium-metal batteries, whether shipped on their own, packed with equipment, or contained inside
equipment, are regulated under Class 9 due to fire risk from thermal runaway. Requirements vary by battery
watt-hour (Wh) rating and quantity:

- Small consumer-type batteries within regulatory exempt limits, correctly packaged and marked, may move under a
  simplified declaration.
- Larger batteries (above the exempt watt-hour threshold), and any batteries identified as damaged, defective, or
  subject to recall, must be shipped under full Class 9 dangerous goods declaration with UN-tested packaging, and
  require pre-approval from Baobab's dangerous goods desk before booking.
- Damaged or recalled lithium batteries are never accepted for standard road freight without specialist
  hazardous-battery packaging (e.g. approved fire-retardant transport bags/containers) confirmed in advance.

## 2. Environmentally hazardous substances

Substances that are harmful to aquatic life or the environment, even where not otherwise classified as dangerous
goods on health or flammability grounds, fall under Class 9 (marked with the "fish and tree" environmental hazard
mark). This commonly applies to certain pesticides, industrial chemicals, and some paint and coating formulations
that do not otherwise meet Class 3 criteria. The SDS environmental hazards section (Section 12) determines whether
this marking applies.

## 3. Dry ice

Dry ice (solid carbon dioxide), used as a coolant for temperature-sensitive freight, is classified under Class 9 due
to asphyxiation risk from carbon dioxide gas buildup in enclosed spaces. Shipments using dry ice as a coolant must
declare the quantity in kilograms, and Baobab's vehicles carrying dry ice loads are fitted with ventilation
appropriate to the declared quantity.

## 4. Packaging and marking

Class 9 packaging requirements are product-specific rather than uniform across the class, given its catch-all
nature. Baobab's dangerous goods desk confirms the correct packaging standard against the SDS and UN number for
each Class 9 product at time of booking, rather than applying a single default rule.

## 5. Common client base

Baobab's Class 9 volume is driven primarily by electronics distributors and repair/refurbishment businesses
(lithium batteries), agricultural chemical suppliers (environmentally hazardous formulations), and the
pharmaceutical/medical cold-chain sector (dry ice for temperature-controlled shipments).
