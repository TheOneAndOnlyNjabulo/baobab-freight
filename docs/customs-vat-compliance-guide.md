# Customs and VAT Compliance Guide

Baobab Freight & Logistics (Pty) Ltd operates as a registered clearing and forwarding agent across South Africa and
the SADC region. This guide summarises the customs duty, import/export VAT, and SARS eFiling obligations that apply
to shipments Baobab handles on behalf of clients. It is not a substitute for a customs broker's formal advice on any
specific consignment — Baobab's own in-house brokerage team should be consulted for HS code disputes, tariff
rulings, or duty valuation queries.

## 1. Import VAT

Import VAT is levied at the standard rate (15%) on the customs value of goods entering South Africa, calculated as
the sum of the Free On Board (FOB) value, freight and insurance to the South African border, plus any customs duty
payable. Import VAT is collected by SARS at the point of clearance and is separate from the customs duty itself.
VAT-registered importers can generally claim this back as input VAT on their next VAT201 return, provided the goods
are used for taxable supplies and the importer holds a valid SAD 500 (Customs Declaration) reflecting their VAT
number.

## 2. Export VAT

Exports of goods from South Africa are zero-rated for VAT purposes under Section 11 of the VAT Act, provided the
exporter retains the required documentary proof: the SAD 500 export declaration, a copy of the commercial invoice,
and proof that the goods physically left South Africa (a stamped road manifest or, for cross-border consignments,
confirmation from the exit border post). Baobab retains copies of border-stamped documentation for 5 years in line
with SARS record-keeping requirements and can supply these to clients on request for their own VAT audit trail.

## 3. HS codes and tariff classification

Every consignment must be classified under the correct Harmonized System (HS) code before a customs declaration can
be lodged. The HS code determines the applicable customs duty rate, whether the goods require an import/export
permit, and whether any trade agreement preference (e.g. SADC Protocol on Trade, or the AfCFTA) can be applied to
reduce or eliminate duty. Misclassification is one of the most common causes of clearance delay and can trigger
SARS penalties of up to 200% of the duty evaded in cases of deliberate misdeclaration. Clients should provide as
much product detail as possible (material composition, function, end use) so Baobab's brokerage team can classify
correctly on first submission.

## 4. Customs duty

Customs duty rates vary by HS code and country of origin. Preferential rates apply to goods originating within SADC
member states where a valid SADC Certificate of Origin (Movement Certificate) accompanies the shipment. Where no
preferential agreement applies, the general (Most Favoured Nation) rate published in Schedule 1 of the Customs and
Excise Act applies. Baobab does not pay duty on a client's behalf without a signed disbursement authority and
pre-funded deposit, except for existing account-holders on approved credit terms.

## 5. SARS eFiling and declaration process

All customs declarations are lodged electronically via SARS eFiling / the Electronic Data Interchange (EDI)
gateway. Baobab's brokerage division submits declarations under its own SARS-registered clearing agent code on
behalf of clients, referencing the client's importer/exporter code. Clients remain the "declarant of record" and
are legally responsible for the accuracy of the information supplied to Baobab, even where Baobab prepares and
submits the declaration.

## 6. Required supporting documents

A complete customs file for import clearance typically includes: commercial invoice, packing list, bill of
lading/road manifest, SAD 500, Certificate of Origin (where a preference is claimed), and any permits required for
controlled or restricted goods (see the Cross-Border Documentation Checklist series for SADC-specific permit
requirements). Missing documentation is the single largest cause of clearance delay on Baobab's SADC corridors.
