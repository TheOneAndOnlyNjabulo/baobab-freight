# Cross-Border Documentation Checklist — Zimbabwe

Requirements specific to the Beitbridge corridor, supplementing the Cross-Border Documentation Checklist —
Overview.

## 1. Import documentation required by ZIMRA

- Bill of entry, lodged electronically through ZIMRA's ASYCUDA system by Baobab's Beitbridge clearing agent
- Import permit, for controlled categories (see section 3 below)
- Certificate of Origin, where a SADC preference is claimed
- Commercial invoice and packing list

## 2. Vehicle documentation

- Temporary Import Permit (TIP) for the truck-tractor and trailer, issued at the border for the duration of the
  trip
- Third-party insurance valid in Zimbabwe (Baobab's fleet carries regional cover; owner-driver subcontractors must
  confirm their own cover extends to Zimbabwe before being assigned a Zimbabwe corridor load)
- Carbon tax and toll fee receipts, collected at designated points along the route

## 3. Permit-controlled categories

Second-hand vehicles and vehicle parts, agricultural equipment, and certain telecommunications equipment require an
import permit issued in advance by the relevant Zimbabwean ministry. Shipments in these categories booked without a
permit already secured will be held at Beitbridge pending the client obtaining one — this is treated as a
documentation-caused delay, not a Baobab service failure, under the Delivery SLA Policy exclusions.

## 4. Currency and payment note

Duty and VAT assessments at Zimbabwean clearance are calculated in USD. Baobab's Beitbridge agent can facilitate
duty payment on behalf of account clients against a pre-agreed disbursement arrangement; ad hoc clients should
confirm payment method with their account manager before the shipment departs South Africa.

## 5. Common documentation errors on this corridor

The most frequent cause of delay at Beitbridge on Baobab's network is a mismatch between the HS code declared on
the South African export SAD 500 and the code used on the Zimbabwean import bill of entry — these should be
confirmed as consistent by Baobab's brokerage team before the SAD 500 is lodged, not corrected after the truck has
already reached the border.
