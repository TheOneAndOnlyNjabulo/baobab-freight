# Delivery SLA — Zambia Corridor

Guaranteed transit windows for the South Africa ↔ Zambia corridor, routed via Zimbabwe and the Chirundu border
post. Penalty terms are set out in the SLA Policy — General Terms and Penalties document.

## 1. Route and border crossings

Zambia-bound traffic transits through Zimbabwe, crossing into South Africa's neighbour at Beitbridge and into
Zambia at Chirundu (Zimbabwe/Zambia border, on the Zambezi). Chirundu is a "one-stop border post," meaning both
countries' customs and immigration formalities are processed at a single facility, which generally reduces
clearance time relative to older dual-post crossings — but the corridor still involves two full border clearances
(Beitbridge, then Chirundu) on a single trip.

## 2. Guaranteed transit times

- Johannesburg ↔ Lusaka: 5 business days
- Johannesburg ↔ Ndola (Copperbelt): 6 business days
- Durban ↔ Lusaka: 6 business days

## 3. Zambia Revenue Authority (ZRA) clearance

Import clearance into Zambia is handled by the Zambia Revenue Authority. Baobab's Lusaka partner agent manages ZRA
clearance for account clients, including the ASYCUDA World electronic declaration required for all commercial
imports. Mining and copperbelt-industry shipments (equipment, spare parts) are common on this corridor and often
qualify for duty rebates under Zambian investment incentive schemes, subject to the client holding a valid
investment certificate.

## 4. Transit-through-Zimbabwe documentation

Because this corridor transits Zimbabwe, a Zimbabwean transit bond (or carnet) is required in addition to the
documentation covered in the Cross-Border Documentation Checklist — Zimbabwe. Baobab arranges the transit bond as
part of the standard Zambia corridor booking; clients do not need to arrange this separately.

## 5. Risk factors affecting the guarantee

This is Baobab's longest standard corridor and carries the highest exposure to cumulative border delay across two
separate crossings. The transit times above already include a larger buffer than domestic or single-border routes.
Fuel availability disruptions in Zambia, when they occur, are treated as a force majeure exclusion under the
General Terms.
