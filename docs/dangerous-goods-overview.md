# Dangerous Goods Handling Policy — Overview

Baobab Freight & Logistics transports dangerous goods (hazardous materials) on designated road freight services in
line with South Africa's National Road Traffic Act and Regulations (Act 93 of 1996), and the SADC region's
respective national equivalents. This overview covers classification, documentation, packaging, and driver
requirements that apply to all hazard classes; class-specific requirements are set out in the Class 3
(Flammable Liquids), Class 8 (Corrosives), and Class 9 (Miscellaneous) documents.

## 1. Classification system

Dangerous goods are classified into nine United Nations hazard classes, several with subdivisions, per the UN
Recommendations on the Transport of Dangerous Goods (the "Orange Book") and as adopted locally under SANS 10228
(The identification and classification of dangerous substances and goods). Every dangerous goods consignment must
be assigned a UN number, proper shipping name, hazard class, and packing group (I = high danger, II = medium
danger, III = low danger) before Baobab will accept the booking.

## 2. Safety Data Sheets (SDS/MSDS)

A current Safety Data Sheet (SDS, formerly referred to as an MSDS) must accompany every dangerous goods booking,
supplied by the client at time of quote request — not at collection. The SDS must be for the specific product being
shipped, not a generic product-family document, and must be less than 5 years old or reissued to the current
16-section GHS-aligned format. Baobab's dispatch team will not schedule a collection for an unclassified or
undocumented hazardous product.

## 3. Packaging requirements

Dangerous goods must be packed in UN-approved packaging appropriate to the assigned packing group, tested and
marked in accordance with SANS 10231 (Transport of dangerous goods — Packing for road and rail transport). Baobab's
drivers are trained to visually reject packaging that is damaged, unmarked, or shows signs of leakage prior to
loading. Repackaging is the client's responsibility — Baobab drivers do not repackage or transfer dangerous goods
between containers.

## 4. Placarding and vehicle marking

Vehicles carrying dangerous goods above the regulatory exempt quantity threshold must display the correct hazard
class placards and, where applicable, the UN number panel, visible from all four sides of the vehicle. Baobab's
fleet carries a standard placard kit and dispatch confirms the correct placard set is fitted before departure based
on the declared hazard class(es) of the consolidated load.

## 5. Driver competency

Drivers assigned to dangerous goods routes hold a valid dangerous goods driving permit as required under the
National Road Traffic Regulations, and complete Baobab's internal refresher training annually, covering emergency
response basics, spill containment, and the use of the Emergency Information for Road Transport (Transport
Emergency Card / TREM Card) that must accompany every hazardous consignment.

## 6. Incompatible goods and segregation

Different hazard classes may not be consolidated on the same vehicle unless segregation rules (based on the
compatibility groups in SANS 10228) are satisfied — for example, oxidizing agents (Class 5) are never loaded with
flammable liquids (Class 3) on the same vehicle. Baobab's load-planning system checks hazard class compatibility
automatically before a consolidated dangerous goods load is confirmed.

## 7. Emergency response

Every dangerous goods vehicle carries a spill kit appropriate to the hazard classes on board, and the driver carries
a TREM Card with 24-hour emergency contact numbers for both Baobab's operations centre and the product
manufacturer/supplier. In the event of an incident, the driver's first action is to secure the scene and contact
Baobab's operations centre, who will activate the emergency response plan for the specific hazard class involved.
