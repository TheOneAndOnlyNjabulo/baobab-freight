# Delivery SLA — Domestic South African Routes

Guaranteed transit windows for road freight moved entirely within South Africa. Penalty terms are set out in the
SLA Policy — General Terms and Penalties document; this document covers route-specific transit times only.

## 1. Metro-to-metro trunk routes

- Johannesburg ↔ Cape Town: 2 business days
- Johannesburg ↔ Durban: 1 business day
- Johannesburg ↔ Port Elizabeth (Gqeberha): 2 business days
- Johannesburg ↔ Bloemfontein: 1 business day
- Cape Town ↔ Durban: 3 business days
- Cape Town ↔ Port Elizabeth: 1 business day
- Durban ↔ Port Elizabeth: 2 business days

## 2. Secondary and regional centres

- Johannesburg ↔ Polokwane: 1 business day
- Johannesburg ↔ Nelspruit (Mbombela): 1 business day
- Johannesburg ↔ Kimberley: 1 business day
- Johannesburg ↔ East London: 2 business days
- Durban ↔ Pietermaritzburg: same business day (collection before 12:00 only)

## 3. Rural and outlying delivery points

Deliveries to addresses more than 50km from a Baobab depot or agent point add 1 business day to the applicable
trunk-route transit time above. Farms, mine sites, and industrial parks without a formal street address require the
consignee to supply GPS coordinates at booking; failure to do so may result in a delivery attempt failure that is
excluded from the SLA guarantee under the "consignee unavailability" exclusion.

## 4. Same-day and express options

Same-day delivery is available on a limited set of metro routes (Johannesburg–Pretoria, Durban–Pietermaritzburg,
Cape Town–Stellenbosch) for collections booked and confirmed before 10:00. Same-day bookings are quoted separately
and are not covered by the standard multi-day guarantee structure — express-specific terms are provided at time of
booking.

## 5. Peak season adjustment

Between 1 December and 15 January, and during the week preceding major public holidays, published transit times
increase by 1 business day across all domestic routes due to volume. This adjustment is communicated to account
clients in advance each year and does not constitute an SLA breach.
