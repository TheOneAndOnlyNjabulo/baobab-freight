# Incoterms Explainer — FOB, CIF, EXW, DAP

Incoterms (International Commercial Terms), published by the International Chamber of Commerce, define which party
— buyer or seller — is responsible for transport costs, insurance, and risk at each stage of a shipment's journey.
Baobab Freight & Logistics quotes and books freight according to the Incoterm specified on the client's purchase
order or sales contract. Getting the Incoterm wrong on a booking is one of the most common sources of billing
disputes, so this guide explains the four terms Baobab handles most frequently on its SA/SADC road freight lanes.

## 1. EXW — Ex Works

Under EXW, the seller's only obligation is to make the goods available at their own premises. The buyer (or the
buyer's appointed carrier — often Baobab, booked directly by the buyer) is responsible for loading, all transport
costs, export clearance, import clearance, and insurance from the seller's door onward. EXW places the maximum
responsibility on the buyer. Baobab requires an EXW client to supply their own export documentation or to
appoint Baobab's brokerage division separately (at additional cost) to handle clearance on their behalf.

## 2. FOB — Free On Board

FOB is a maritime/inland-waterway term strictly speaking, but is used informally in inland freight contexts to mean
the seller bears cost and risk up to a named point of departure (e.g. loading onto the truck or at the port of
export), after which the buyer bears cost and risk. For Baobab's purposes, an FOB booking typically means the
consignor has arranged and paid for transport to the port/border, and Baobab is booked by the consignee for the
onward leg. Risk transfers at the point named in the contract, not at Baobab's depot — this matters for insurance
claims, since Baobab's transit insurance only covers the leg it is contracted to move.

## 3. CIF — Cost, Insurance and Freight

Under CIF, the seller pays for freight and insurance to the named destination port, but risk transfers to the buyer
once goods are loaded at origin — meaning the buyer bears the risk of loss even though the seller paid for the
insurance policy. When Baobab is booked as the destination-side carrier on a CIF shipment, the consignee should
confirm the seller's insurance certificate covers the inland leg Baobab performs, or arrange supplementary cover,
since standard marine CIF insurance sometimes stops at the port of discharge.

## 4. DAP — Delivered At Place

DAP places the most responsibility on the seller: the seller arranges and pays for transport (and insurance, if
elected) all the way to a named destination, and bears the risk until the goods arrive there, ready for unloading
by the buyer. Import clearance and duties at destination remain the buyer's responsibility unless the contract
specifies DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) instead. Most of Baobab's cross-border SADC deliveries booked directly by South
African exporters use DAP terms, with Baobab handling the full door-to-door movement and the buyer handling import
clearance on arrival.

## 5. Choosing the right term

Clients should confirm the Incoterm in writing on every booking. Baobab's rate quotes assume the Incoterm stated on
the booking form determines who Baobab invoices (consignor or consignee) and what scope of service (clearance,
insurance, last-mile delivery) is included. A mismatch between the stated Incoterm and the actual service requested
is the most common cause of invoice disputes on cross-border loads.
