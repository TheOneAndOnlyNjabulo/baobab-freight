# Cargo Insurance and Carrier Liability

This document explains the difference between Baobab Freight & Logistics' standard carrier liability and optional
supplementary cargo insurance, and how the two interact when a damage or loss claim is made.

## 1. Standard carrier liability vs. insurance

Every shipment moved by Baobab is automatically covered by Baobab's standard carrier liability, which is limited
(see the Damaged Goods and Lost Goods claims process documents for the specific cap). This is not insurance in the
conventional sense — it is a contractual limitation of Baobab's own liability as carrier, and it applies whether or
not the client has purchased anything additional. Supplementary cargo insurance is a separate, optional product
that covers the client for the difference between the standard liability cap and the full declared value of the
goods.

## 2. Arranging supplementary cargo insurance

Clients can request supplementary cargo insurance at time of booking by declaring the full value of the goods and
selecting the insurance option on the booking form or through their account manager. The premium is calculated as a
percentage of declared value and is added to the freight invoice. Insurance must be arranged before collection —
Baobab cannot backdate cover to a shipment already in transit.

## 3. What is and isn't covered

Supplementary cargo insurance covers physical loss or damage during transit, including collection and delivery
handling. It does not cover: consequential loss (e.g. lost sales, contractual penalties owed by the client to their
own customer), goods inadequately packaged for the mode of transport, or goods falling within an excluded category
(perishables without temperature-controlled booking, cash, and specified high-value electronics above a threshold
requiring separate declaration).

## 4. Claims process when insurance is in place

Where supplementary insurance was purchased, the client still follows the standard Damaged Goods or Lost Goods
claims process for initial reporting. Baobab's claims team will assess the claim against both the standard carrier
liability and the insurance policy, and settle up to the full insured value rather than the standard liability cap.

## 5. Dangerous goods and insurance

Shipments of dangerous goods (see the Dangerous Goods Handling Policy series) carry a separate insurance loading
due to elevated risk. Clients moving hazardous materials should confirm insurance terms with their account manager
before booking, as standard cargo insurance terms may not automatically extend to hazard-class cargo without this
confirmation.
