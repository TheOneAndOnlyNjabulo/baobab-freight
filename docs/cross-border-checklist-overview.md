# Cross-Border Documentation Checklist — Overview

Baobab Freight & Logistics moves freight across four SADC land borders: Zimbabwe (Beitbridge), Botswana
(Skilpadshek/Pioneer Gate and Groblersbrug/Martin's Drift), Zambia (via Zimbabwe and Chirundu), and Mozambique
(Lebombo/Ressano Garcia and Kosi Bay). This overview covers documentation common to all four corridors;
country-specific requirements are detailed in the per-country checklist documents.

## 1. Core document set required for every cross-border consignment

- Commercial invoice (in the currency of sale, showing full product description and value)
- Packing list
- Road manifest / waybill
- SAD 500 customs declaration (South African export leg)
- Certificate of Origin, where a SADC or other trade preference is being claimed
- Any product-specific permit (see individual country checklists)

## 2. SADC Certificate of Origin

Goods of South African (or other SADC member state) origin qualifying under the SADC Protocol on Trade can move at
preferential (reduced or nil) duty rates in the destination country, provided a valid Movement Certificate /
Certificate of Origin is presented. Baobab's brokerage team can advise whether a specific product qualifies as
"originating" under the rules of origin, which depend on where inputs were sourced and how much local processing
occurred, not simply where the goods were assembled.

## 3. Cross-Border Road Transport Agency (C-BRTA) permit

Vehicles operating cross-border freight services into SADC countries require a valid cross-border road transport
permit issued under the Cross-Border Road Transport Act. Baobab's fleet holds valid C-BRTA permits for all four
corridors it services; this is a vehicle/operator-level requirement and does not need to be arranged separately by
clients.

## 4. Vehicle and driver documents

Every cross-border trip carries: the vehicle's registration and roadworthy documentation, the driver's passport
(valid for the trip duration plus a margin), the driver's professional driving permit (PrDP) where required for the
class of goods, and — for dangerous goods loads — the documents described in the Dangerous Goods Handling Policy
series.

## 5. General advice for clients

The single biggest driver of cross-border delay on Baobab's network is incomplete documentation supplied by the
client before collection, not border congestion itself. Clients should submit final commercial invoices and any
permits at least 24 hours before a scheduled cross-border collection so Baobab's brokerage team can pre-lodge
customs declarations where the destination country's systems support it.
